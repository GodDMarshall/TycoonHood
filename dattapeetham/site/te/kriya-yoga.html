    Datta Kriya Yoga

    A science of meditation and a skilful way of living: the Kriya Yoga of Adi Guru Dattatreya, taught by Sri Swamiji to seekers in the East and the West since 1966.

    What is Datta Kriya Yoga

    Union, through action

    Derived from the Sanskrit yuj, yoga signifies union: the individual soul (jeevatma) merging with the Universal Soul (Paramatma), Sachchidananda Swaroopa. Kriya denotes action. Kriya Yoga channels the life-force energy (praana) through mental processes to achieve this union, starting with pranayama, the control of breath.

    According to the Markandeya Purana, the eternal Guru Dattatreya taught this mystic Kriya Yoga to Subramanya Swamy. Because it was bestowed by Lord Dattatreya, it is known as Datta Kriya Yoga. Vaamadeva, Jadabharata, Sukadeva, Parasurama, Prahlada, Alarka and Kartaviryarjuna all became great Yogis through it.

    Sri Swamiji teaches Datta Kriya Yoga in the lineage of Adi Guru Dattatreya, drawing inspiration from his mother and Guru, Sri Jayalakshmi Mata, a Master of Raja Yoga and Sree Vidya.

    The Guru Parampara

    Datta Kriya Yoga is both a science of meditation and a skilful way of living.

    ॐ

    Adhyaatma yogaadhigamena devam
    Matvaamdheero harshashokau jahaati

    “The intelligent man, having known the Effulgent One by means of Adhyatma Yoga (meditation on the inner self), abandons both joy and sorrow.”

    Kathopanishad
    ॐ

    Te dhyaana yogaanugataa apashyan
    Devaatmashaktim svagunaairnigoodham

    “The Rishis (seers) absorbed in Dhyana Yoga (the yoga of meditation) realized the Soul Power of the Supreme concealed by the gunas (qualities).”

    Shvetaashvatara Upanishad
    ॐ

    Dhyaanaavasthita tadgatena manasaa pashyanti yam yoginah

    “Whom the Yogis behold with their minds engrossed in THAT, through meditation.”

    Concluding Dhyana Shloka, Srimad Bhagavad Gita

    Every chapter of the Bhagavad Gita is termed “yoga”, and the entire Gita is designated as Yoga Shastra.

    Breath awareness

    Focused breath, a quiet mind

    Focused yogic breathing quietens the mind and directs the flow of vital energy (prana) through the entire body. Prana is the vital life force that sustains and awakens our subtle energy system.

    InhaleExhale

    Ancient yogis viewed lifespan in terms of breaths, with rapid breathing linked to shorter lifespans. Datta Kriya Yoga offers accessible practices to improve health by regulating breath, regardless of physical condition or lifestyle.

    The circle is illustrative, a quiet aid to breath awareness. It is not a timed exercise; learn the techniques from a Datta Kriya Yoga teacher.

    The practice

    A blend of Hatha and Raja Yoga

    Sri Swamiji has renewed the Kriya Yoga principle: the encapsulation of Hatha Yoga, the exercise-based yoga practice of today, and Raja Yoga, which involves mind control through breath control.

    Mental, pranic and physical

    Sri Swamiji selects specific mental, pranic and physical techniques to address the imbalances of modern lifestyles and to promote unity in diversity. The purpose: to minimise afflictions of body and mind and improve single-pointed concentration.

    For the ordinary householder, the way to know and learn Yoga is Datta Kriya Yoga. It deals with:

    • Restraint of breath
    • Various techniques of breathing
    • Relaxation
    • Withdrawal of the sense organs
    • Fixing one’s attention on a particular deity or object, for concentration and contemplation

    Five componentsThe elements of a practice

    1. 01

      Naadi Shuddhi Vyayama

      Purifying the subtle nerves

      Techniques unique to Datta Kriya Yoga that purify the subtle nerves. Suitable for all age groups.

    2. 02

      Asanas

      Postures

      Yoga postures that enhance flexibility and build strength. Postures like the Tree Pose aid concentration.

    3. 03

      Pranayama

      Breath control

      Specific breath-control techniques, followed by purification actions (kriyas) specific only to Datta Kriya Yoga.

    4. 04

      Mudras

      Gestures

      Gestures that accompany the pranayamas, directing vital energy to specific areas of the body.

    5. 05

      Dhyana

      Meditation

      Meditation, for concentration and contemplation. Datta Kriya Yoga is, at heart, a science of meditation.

    Benefits

    Balance for body, mind & spirit

    Regular practice leads to a joyful and blissful life. Datta Kriya Yoga is suitable for all age groups, and you don’t need any specific religious beliefs to benefit from it.

    • Balance body, mind & spirit

      Promotes holistic well-being by harmonising the physical, mental and spiritual aspects of our lives.

    • Eliminate toxins & boost immunity

      Asanas, kriyas and pranayama help detoxify the body through sweat and respiration, enhancing the immune system.

    • Respiration, energy & vitality

      Asanas, pranayama and mudras strengthen the respiratory system and invigorate vital energy.

    • Strength & flexibility

      The yoga asanas and Nadi Shuddhi exercises enhance flexibility and build strength.

    • Concentration & focus

      Postures like the Tree Pose, along with pranayama and meditation, boost concentration and focus.

    • Core strength

      Specific asanas and pranayama practices develop and strengthen the core muscles.

    Guinness World Record · 10 July 2016

    The largest breathing lesson

    At India Community Center, Milpitas, California, USA, in an event organised by Dashavatara Yoga Center (USA), Sri Swamiji led the largest pranayama (breathing exercise) lesson in a single location.

    He explained the role of breathing in reducing stress and improving health: the relation between nerves, nerve centres and breathing patterns, the importance of long breath with sustained focus, and how systematic breathing helps with ailments and lifestyle diseases.

    Sri Swamiji has experimented with mudras, kriyas and Nadi Shuddhi exercises for several decades; his scientific approach to pranayama has benefited millions of people the world over.

    DKY International Centre

    Datta Kriya Yoga International Centre

    Accredited as a “Yoga Training Centre” by the Yoga Certification Board, Ministry of AYUSH, Government of India. Certificate No. YCB/20/YC/000019

    DKYIC has taken up the work of sharing Sri Swamiji’s teachings around the globe. To present the theory and practice of Datta Kriya Yoga systematically, short and long-term courses are conducted at the DKYIC in Mysuru and at a few selected places in India and abroad. Earnest students from all over India and abroad attend.

    Its courses are formulated to suit fast-thinking, active youth under the stress of modern lifestyles, and the principal factors of yogic practice are synchronised with modern scientific detail such as physiology. Classes are held in person and online.

    Prayers to the Guru

    Three prayers from the Datta Kriya Yoga tradition, with their meanings.

    Guru Paduka Dhyana Shlokam

    śrī bhūyuta śrī hari cihnitābhyām agastya rekhādibhirancitābhyām |
    nigūdha bījākṣara mudritābhyām namo namah(s) sadguru pādukābhyām ||

    Meaning. I salute those sacred Sadguru padukas on which are depicted symbolically Lord Vishnu along with Sree Devi (Goddess of Wealth) and Bhoodevi (Goddess of Earth), Agastya (Saint) and other lines, and also several seed letters of occult nature.

    Devi Prarthana

    mūlādhāraṁ tathā svādhiṣṭhānaṁ ca manipūrakam
    anāhatam viśuddhākhyam ājñācakram vidurbudhāh
    tavādhāra svarūpāṇī konacakrani pārvāti
    trikona rupini śaktih bindurūpaḥ(s) śivassmrtah
    avinābhāva sambandhah tasmāt bindu trikonayoh ||

    Meaning. The learned Seers say that the transcendental spiritual energy called ‘Kundalini’ resides in Moolaadhaara and pervades the Swadhishtana, Manipooraka, Anahata, Vishuddha and Ajna Chakras (pranic energy distribution centres in the body). The Goddess Parvati, based in these chakras in the form of triangles, energises the chakras. The energy in the form of a triangle has at its centre the Bindu (dot), the form of Siva. Therefore an inseparable relationship exists between Shiva and Shakti.

    Guru Prarthana

    mūlādhāre ganapati vapurdhārinam deśikendram |
    svādhishthāne dhratavidhitanum sādhakoddāra dakṣam |
    jvālāvarṇam rucira manipūrāntare rudramūrtim |
    nādātmānam vitata mahitānāhatākāśa purnām |
    bhāmārdhāngam paramapuruṣākāra gamyam viśuddhe |
    jyotīrūpam vikti rahitam tattvamajñātārāle |
    svātmārāmam dvayavirahitam tam sahasrāra padme |
    dhyāyāmi tvām ganapati gurum saccidānanda rūpam |

    Meaning. To that Supreme Master who resides in the Moolaadhaara Chakra in the form of Lord Ganapathy; who resides in the Swadhishtana Chakra in the form of Lord Brahma, elevating the sadhaka (spiritual practitioner); who abides in the Manipooraka Chakra radiating the redness of fire in the form of Lord Rudra (Shiva); who dwells in the Anahata Chakra and fills all space (akash) with sound; who is resplendent like Lord Ardhanarishwara (Shiva) as the ultimate Purusha (Supreme Soul) in the Vishuddha Chakra; who, located in the Ajna Chakra, illumines without change as the principles of light and wisdom; who, beyond duality, as one without a second, exists in the absolute state of Atman (Self) and Universal Consciousness in the thousand-petal lotus — to Him, the Ganapati Guru, the embodiment of sat-chit-ananda (truth-knowledge-bliss), I offer my prayers again and again.

    Text as published on the Peetham’s Datta Kriya Yoga page. Diacritic encoding slips (ü, ä, ş for ū, ā, ṣ) and obvious typos in the meanings have been corrected; spellings are otherwise unchanged.
