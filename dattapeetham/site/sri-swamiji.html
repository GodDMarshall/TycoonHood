    Sri Ganapathy Sachchidananda Swamiji

    Founder of Avadhoota Datta Peetham, Mysuru — renowned for His profound spiritual teachings, music therapy and dedication to humanitarian service.

    His Life

    A Life of Divine Compassion

    Sri Sri Sri Ganapathy Sachchidananda Swamiji, also known as Sri Swamiji, is a revered spiritual leader and the head of the Avadhoota Datta Peetham in Mysuru, India.

    I Chapter I 1942

    Born on a rock in the Kaveri

    Sri Swamiji was born at the sacred confluence of three rivers — Arkavati, Kaveri and Guptagaamini — at Mekedatu, Karnataka, to the pious Brahmin couple Narasimha Shastry, later known as Sri Narahari Teertha Swami, and Jayalakshmi Maata, on 26 May 1942.

    His actual birth occurred on a rock in the Kaveri river. His mother was in deep meditation, and the child was born covered with holy ash. He was named Satyanarayana. A Muslim saint had predicted to His parents that their child would have supreme wisdom and knowledge, and was destined to uplift mankind.

    II Chapter II 1953

    His mother, His first Guru

    Sri Swamiji’s mother, Sri Jayalakshmi Maata, was His first Guru and initiated Him into spiritual life. She bestowed Sri Vidya and Raja Yoga, and explained the esoteric meanings of the Vedas, Puranas, Mahabharata and Ramayana.

    At the tender age of eleven, in the Anjaneya Temple at Bommeparti, she transmitted spiritual energy — Shaktipaatam — to the child. She attained Maha Samadhi the same year, 1953.

    Satyanarayana completed His schooling like other children, but found that school had nothing to offer Him. He sang bhajans and kirtans for His friends, and gave Yoga classes in small village huts or on river banks while wandering the countryside as a monk.

    The Guru Parampara

    III Chapter III 1966

    Mysuru, and a new name

    In 1966 Sri Swamiji moved to the present site of the Mysuru Ashram on Ooty Road — in those early days only a straw hut in a field. The land was barren and infested with cobras and scorpions. He slept under the trees on the bare ground with these creatures crawling all around Him, and was never harmed.

    It was there, at Mysuru, that He came to be known as Sri Ganapathy Sachchidananda Swamiji. The same year He was initiated into Sanyasa at Srisailam and installed Sri Sachchidanandeshwara at the Mysuru Ashram.

    People come to Him for help with their worldly affairs, to be healed of physical afflictions, or for guidance in their spiritual practice.

    Ashram

    IV Chapter IV

    One God, every faith

    Sri Swamiji prays with equal fervour with Hindus, Christians, Muslims, Jains, Jews, Sikhs, Buddhists and those of other faiths. He urges a Christian to become a more ardent Christian, and a Muslim a more devout Muslim.

    Allah is your name. Buddha is your name.
    Jesus is your name. Jina is your name.
    When people call God you always answer.
    I seek refuge in Sachchidananda.

    From Siveti Shaureeti, Sri Swamiji’s Sanskrit prayer — chanted every day in all the branches of His Ashrams.

    He is a musician, a composer, a linguist and a poet; a doctor, a teacher and a psychiatrist; mother, father, friend and child to His devotees.

    Sri Swamiji unveiled
    Across the world

    A spiritual message carried to almost every country

    As of 2024, Sri Swamiji has made more than one hundred and eighty-three Vishwa Dharma Vijaya Yatras for the establishment of Dharma. His first foreign tour took place in 1976, and He has visited almost all the countries of the world, spreading the message of peace, the oneness of God and universal brotherhood.

    His Mission

    Six streams of one mission

    Avadhoota Datta Peetham was established by Sri Swamiji in 1966 as an international spiritual, cultural and social welfare organisation. Its projects revolve around the intrinsic worth of every single human life.

    Nada

    Bhajans & Healing Music

    More than 3,000 bhajans in eight languages, and Music for Meditation & Healing drawn from the ancient Raga Ragini Vidya — played on the synthesizer He calls the Datta Veena.

    Nada Chikitsa
    Dharma

    Sanatana Dharma Parirakshana

    Temples and Ashrams, the SGS Veda Pathashala, homas and yaagas, care of Gomata and the Gita Mahayajna — keeping the eternal dharma alive.

    On dattapeetham.org
    Yoga

    Datta Kriya Yoga

    A system of Yoga that blends breath control, mind control, body control and universal spiritual wisdom, taught in all the branches of the Ashram.

    Datta Kriya Yoga
    Seva

    Social Activities

    Annadana, SGS Hospital, free medical camps, schools for the needy and the Amma Vodi home for destitute women — service to mankind is service to God.

    Pillars of service
    Nature

    Contribution towards Nature

    Shuka Vana, home to birds of 486 species, and the Kishkindha Moolika Bonsai Garden. “Bonsai trees and birds are Swamiji’s two eyes.”

    On dattapeetham.org
    Wisdom

    Literary Contributions

    Keertanas and bhajans in many languages, and books on spirituality — prominently in Telugu and Kannada — published by the Peetham.

    Sri Swamiji’s Avatara Timeline

    Milestones since 1942

    From a rock in the Kaveri to a concert in a cave: the milestones in the life of Sri Swamiji, as the Peetham records them.

    1. 1942

      Birth of Sri Swamiji on 26 May 1942 — Jyeshta Shuddha Ekadashi of the year Chitrabhanu — at Mekedatu.

    2. 1953

      Shaktipaatam — transmission of energy — from Mother Sri Jayalakshmi Maata; her Maha Samadhi the same year.

    3. 1966

      Ashram established at Mysuru. Sanyasa initiation at Srisailam. Installation of Sri Sachchidanandeshwara.

    4. 1976

      Bhaktimala monthly magazine begins, later published in seven languages. Sri Swamiji’s first foreign tour.

    5. 1978

      SGS Hospital inaugurated in Mysuru to provide free medical services.

    6. 1980

      Sree Lakshmi Narasimha Kshetra established at the Mysuru Ashram.

    7. 1981

      Datta Kriya Yoga facilities introduced at the Mysuru Ashram.

    8. 1984

      Universal Prayer Hall unveiled.

    9. 1985

      Sri Ganapathy Sachchidananda Veda Pathashala founded.

    10. 1986

      Sri Jayalakshmi Maata Nitya Annadana Trust established.

    11. 1987

      School for the study of the Shastras begins.

    12. 1988

      Educational Institution started.

    13. 1989

      Study of Raga Ragini and Dhyana Chikitsa Kacheri restored; an R&D branch on Nada Chikitsa set up; distribution of CDs for the treatment promoted.

    14. 1992

      Ascends the Audumbara Dharma Simhasana.

    15. 1993

      Narahareeshwara installed at Jayalakshmipuram. Eight-month Nada Prasara tour.

    16. 1994

      Vedic examinations begin; Veda Nidhi and Nada Nidhi titles introduced.

    17. 1995

      Pilgrimage to Manasa Sarovara in the Himalayas.

    18. 1996

      Shastra Nidhi title introduced. Raga Ragini Trust formed.

    19. 1998

      Nada Mantapa inaugurated at the Mysuru Ashram.

    20. 1999

      Sri Datta Venkateshwara Kshetram installed.

    21. 2000

      Pillar of Dharma inaugurated.

    22. 2002

      60th birthday celebrations. Re-installation of Kaalaagni Shamana Dattatreya.

    23. 2004

      Sri Datta Vijayananda Teertha Swamiji crowned Junior Pontiff. I-SERVE — Institute for Scientific Research on Vedas — inaugurated.

    24. 2005

      Kishkindha Moolika Bonsai Garden inaugurated.

    25. 2006

      Saptarshi Sarovara and Vishwam Museum inaugurated. Chitike literary work released.

    26. 2012

      70th birthday. Shuka Vana inaugurated. 70-ft Karya Siddhi Hanuman statue consecrated.

    27. 2013

      Honorary Doctorate in Music — Vijayanagara Sri Krishna Devaraya University.

    28. 2015

      Guinness World Records: most people chanting at a single venue, largest music therapy lesson, longest chanting marathon (team).

    29. 2016

      Guinness World Records: largest breathing lesson, largest online video album of people chanting, largest display of bonsai trees. Honorary Doctorate — World Classical Tamil University.

    30. 2017

      Guinness World Records: most bird species in an aviary, largest Hindu Smriti.

    31. 2018

      Guinness World Record: world’s largest special stamp.

    32. 2022

    33. 2023

      Honorary Doctorate in Music — Dr. Gangubai Hanagal Music and Performing Arts University.

    34. 2024

    Sri Bala Swamiji

    Sri Datta Vijayananda Teertha Swamiji

    Often referred to as Sri Bala Swamiji, He is the junior pontiff of Avadhoota Datta Peetham, Mysuru — the spiritual successor to Sri Ganapathy Sachchidananda Swamiji, revered for His wisdom, humility and dedication to spreading the teachings of the Datta tradition.

    He was born in a traditional family to Smt. Saraswati and Sri Subramanyam on 14 January 1977 in Mysuru, and named Sri Harsha Sharma. Harsha had His Vedic education in the Sri Ganapathy Sachchidananda Veda Pathashala, Mysuru. Sri Swamiji identified His good qualities and kept Him under His spiritual tutelage; He has received meticulous training in the Ashram since He was seven years old.

    On 29 May 2004 Sri Swamiji anointed Sri Harsha Sharma as Junior Pontiff in the presence of erudite scholars, giving Him the Sannyasa Deeksha name “Sri Datta Vijayananda Teertha”.

    Mekedatu Padayatra

    Discourse, Chaturmasya and the Padayatra

    Sri Bala Swamiji gives regular discourses on the Vedas, Shastras, Upanishads and Puranas, explaining their deep essence in simple words. Since 2014, on Sri Swamiji’s instruction, He has given a daily commentary on the Yoga Vasishtham. He is also a singer-lyricist, with compositions in Sanskrit, Telugu, Kannada, Tamil and English.

    Every year He commences the Chaturmasya Vrata Deeksha from Guru Purnima, after the Guru Parampara Pooja and Vyaasa Pooja, and spearheads yagnas, homas, discourses, Annadana and free medical camps during the vrata.

    As a prelude to Sri Swamiji’s 80th birthday celebrations, He led a six-day Vishwashanti Padayatra for world peace from Mekedatu, Sri Swamiji’s birthplace, to the Mysuru Ashram — bringing Kaveri water to wash Sri Swamiji’s feet, and supporting five causes:

    • Protection of cows and ambulances for them
    • Free medical camps and treatment for women and children with cancer and other diseases
    • Help for financially weak Vedic schools
    • Help for poor farmers
    • Protecting Nature by planting trees
