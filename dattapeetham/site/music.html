    Nada Chikitsa healing through music

    Sri Ganapathy Sachchidananda Swamiji is widely recognised for His transformative Divya Naama Sankeertana — singing the divine names — and for Healing & Meditation Music that has touched countless lives around the world.

    Nada Chikitsa

    Where melody becomes medicine

    Rooted in ancient Vedic teaching as nāda cikitsā, healing through sound is what the world now calls music therapy. Several decades of deep, intuitive research into the effects of musical notes and rhythms on the human body have given Sri Swamiji a profound knowledge of effective music therapy.

    Coupling this with the ancient Indian science of Raga Ragini Vidya, He plays Healing Music for audiences across the world. In concert He alternates between melodies that relax and melodies that energise, accompanied by musicians on classical Indian instruments.

    “Only a Nadayogi adept in the aspects of traditional as well as the modern sciences is able to blend the art of music with healing music therapeutic medicine and perform Nada Chikitsa.”

    Avadhoota Datta Peetham
    01

    The Naadi system

    The subtle body comprises 72,000 Naadis; 14 are considered most significant for carrying praana from the subtle to the physical body. A disruption in the Naadis can lead to physical or mental ailments.

    02

    Sound and praana

    The flow of sound vibration — naada — parallels the flow of praana. Specific raagas are associated with healing specific organs of the body, aligned with the flow of praana through the Naadis.

    03

    Mind and thought

    Thoughts manifest as sound in the mind. Healing music introduces higher vibrations that calm, and eventually control, the daily influx of thoughts, preparing the mind for meditation.

    Body, mind and soul

    The effects the Peetham describes

    When Nada, the pure sound of melody, is approached reverentially and absorbed with total concentration, its vibrations “clear the dust off the mirror-like mind”, and the inherent divinity in man is reflected.

    At the physical level
    Lowered blood pressure, a stabilised heart rate, improved motor skills and relief from pain.
    At the mental level
    Reduced depression and anxiety, strengthened memory and enhanced intellectual activity.
    Raga Ragini Vidya

    An ocean of ragas

    Sri Swamiji understands the ancient music system of Raga-Ragini, has unparalleled access to melodies, and has created many new ragas using the ancient system within present musicology. Because His compositions are so prolific, the title Sagara — ocean — was given to express this form of Raga-Ragini.

    Ragas composed by Sri Swamiji

    Sri Swamiji has created numerous new ragas within the framework of the ancient system. Among them:

    • Eeshanana
    • Anagha
    • Puttu Gam Sri

    The Datta Veena

    Sri Swamiji performs on a synthesizer programmed with over 1,000 personally selected sounds, which He fondly calls the Datta Veena, accompanied by master musicians.

    Raga Sagara concerts

    Music for Meditation & Healing

    Sri Swamiji has been giving Healing Music Concerts — Music for Meditation — for several decades, accompanied by world-renowned musicians. Each concert carries its own Raga Sagara name.

    Every ticket also serves the world

    Sri Swamiji, in His boundless compassion, has resolved to donate the proceeds from all upcoming concert ticket sales towards relief efforts for natural calamities. Attending a Music for Meditation & Healing session brings a dual benefit: personal healing, and support for natural disaster relief.

    Divya Naama Sankeertana

    Bhajans: singing the divine names

    Sri Swamiji is a gifted composer and singer — a versatile Vaggeyakaara, lyricist and composer in many languages, adept at crafting melodies and setting rhythms. He has composed more than 3,000 bhajans in praise of various deities.

    He passionately advocates Divya Naama Sankeertana as a means to transcend fear and find inner peace, and has inspired tens of thousands of devotees on the path of devotional singing. His sessions enthral a musical layman and a Carnatic scholar alike.

    The Hall of Musical Tones · Raga Ragini Samudaya Bhavan

    Nada Mantapa

    Inaugurated by Sri Swamiji on 31 May 1998, this purpose-built auditorium at the Mysuru Ashram has hosted thousands of concerts. It is equipped with state-of-the-art sound, lighting and theatrical systems.

    Born of a Ramayana story

    The fan-shaped design was inspired by an episode Sri Swamiji narrates: Lord Rama, seeking Seetha in the forest, was fanned by Hanuman with a palm leaf. The auditorium takes the shape of that palm-leaf fan, with its entrance facing east towards the sacred Chamundi hills.

    A hall that listens

    Large portraits of the great composers line the walls. When a composer’s work is performed, the lights before that portrait illuminate — when Saint Tyagaraja’s “Samaja Varagamana” is sung, Tyagaraja’s portrait glows.

    At the entrance stand sculpted deities associated with music: Chaitanya Maha Prabhu, Narada Muni, Garuda, Vishwaksena, Anjaneya, Tumburu, Hayagreeva, Nandi, Bhringi and Ganapati.
