    Our story · since 1966

    From a straw hut to an international institution

    In 1966, Sri Swamiji moved to the present site of the Mysuru Ashram on Ooty Road, which in the early days was only a straw hut in a field. The land was barren and infested with cobras and scorpions; Sri Swamiji slept under the trees on the bare ground with these creatures crawling all around Him. He was never harmed.

    It was there, at Mysuru, that Sri Swamiji came to be known as Sri Ganapathy Sachchidananda Swamiji. People came to His Holiness for help with their worldly affairs, to be healed of physical afflictions, or for guidance in their spiritual practice.

    Where the Datta Temple stands today, a large stage once occupied the place. It was called Saraswati Peetha, and sometimes Narayana Peetha. After the Datta Temple was built, the Ashrama began to be called Avadhoota Datta Peetham.

    Popularly called Sri Ganapathy Sachchidananda Ashram, the Peetham is the headquarters of Sri Swamiji’s mission. It has grown over the years to an impressive stature and encompasses many aspects of the spiritual life: here one can study Yoga, the Vedas and Sanskrit, bhajans, music and much more.

    The Ashram through the years

    1966 — today

    Milestones from the Peetham’s own records, from the first installation to the newest shrines.

    Sri Swamiji’s Avatara Timeline
    1. 1966

      A straw hut in a field

      Sri Swamiji moves to the present site on Ooty Road — then only a straw hut in a field, on barren land. The Ashram is established and Sri Sachchidanandeshwara is installed.

    2. 1978

      The Datta Temple

      On 9 June 1978, during a week-long ceremony, Lord Datta is installed as Sri Kaalaagni Shamana Dattatreya. After the Datta Temple was built, the Ashrama began to be called Avadhoota Datta Peetham. The SGS Hospital opens for free medical services.

    3. 1984

      Prayer Hall and Pathashala

      The Universal Prayer Hall is unveiled; in 1985 the SGS Veda Pathashala is founded to teach the Vedas in the traditional Gurukula system.

    4. 1998

      Nada Mantapa

      Sri Swamiji inaugurates Nada Mantapa, the Hall of Musical Tones, on 31 May 1998. Sri Datta Venkateshwara Kshetram follows in 1999 and the Pillar of Dharma in 2000.

    5. 2002

      The Datta idol re-consecrated

      On Rama Navami, 21 April 2002, the idol of Lord Datta is relocated to the centre of the Prayer Hall and re-consecrated.

    6. 2005

      Gardens and a museum

      The Kishkindha Moolika Bonsai Garden opens in 2005, followed in 2006 by the Vishwam Museum and Saptarshi Sarovara.

    7. 2012

      Shuka Vana and Karya Siddhi Hanuman

      In Sri Swamiji’s 70th year, Shuka Vana, the rehabilitation centre for birds, is founded and the 70-foot Karya Siddhi Hanuman statue is consecrated.

    8. 2016

      Ashta Bhairava

      On 14 December 2016 eight Bhairava idols are consecrated as per Shaiva Agama rites, where a tunnel once led towards Chamundi Hill when the Ashrama started in 1966.

    9. 2023

      Rajarajeshwari and Hari Sannidhi

      On 28 December 2023 Sri Swamiji consecrates the temple of Mother Goddess Rajarajeshwari and unveils Hari Sannidhi, home to 2,108 rare Salagramas.

    10. Today

      An international institution

      More than 80 branches across India — including 16 Ashrams for the 16 incarnations of Lord Dattatreya — and 15 centres abroad, in the UK, USA, Malaysia, Europe, Trinidad, Canada and Australia.

    Our mission, in our own words

    What the Peetham stands for

    Avadhoota Datta Peetham is an international spiritual, cultural and social welfare organization established by His Holiness Sri Ganapathy Sachchidananda Swamiji in 1966. Its many projects revolve upon the recognition of the intrinsic worth of every single human life.

    At the heart of the Peetham

    Lord Dattatreya, the eternal teacher

    At the heart of the Peetham is Lord Dattatreya, the presiding deity who embodies the Sadguru, or Master Teacher, principle. Dattatreya the eternal teacher is the manifestation of the Triple Principle of Sat (Existence), Chit (Knowledge) and Ananda (Bliss).

    He guides seekers on their journey from the shadows of the material world to the illumination of the inner soul. As Sri Swamiji propagates the teachings of Dattatreya Avadhoota, a Datta Temple has been built in the Ashram — the first of the several Datta temples established in India and abroad.

    • “Do all such deeds to others, which you expect from others.”
    • “Try to identify divinity in every being.”

    Lord Dattatreya’s message of Universal Brotherhood

    Spiritual, moral and social upliftment

    “Avadhoota Datta Peetham is a world renowned institution for the social, moral and spiritual welfare of mankind.” Sri Swamiji reminds people of their social responsibility towards their fellow beings.

    Sri Swamiji’s mission

    Preserving the Vedic tradition

    “The Vedas are our priceless inheritance from the remote past and they need to be preserved and propagated.” The Pathashala teaches all four Vedas, and Veda examinations are held every year on a national scale.

    Teachings

    Service to mankind is service to God

    Sri Swamiji reminds us time and again that service to mankind is service to God — through hospitals and medical camps, schools, and Annadanam, by which all Ashram visitors are fed free.

    Seva

    Open to all

    Sri Swamiji welcomes everyone, including agnostics, atheists and people of any faith. The motto is to serve humanity without the barricades of caste, creed, race or religion.

    Visit the Ashram

    Music and devotion

    Bhajan singing is one of the main methods Sri Swamiji propagates, so Divya Naama Sankeertana is a daily part of Ashram life — and music, practised with deep concentration, becomes a therapy.

    Nada Chikitsa

    Reverence for nature

    “Bonsai trees and birds are my two eyes. Naada is my voice. My breath is the Veda.” — Sri Swamiji. Sri Swamiji promotes with zeal the preservation of flora and fauna: the Ashram houses Shuka Vana, the Bonsai garden, a Gau Shala and gardens of trees linked to the Rashis and Nakshatras.

    The lineage of the masters

    Guru Parampara

    Guru Parampara is the continuous succession, or lineage, of Gurus. The Peetham’s Guru Parampara prayer begins with salutations to Lord Ganapathy and Mother Saraswathy, then to the lineage of Gurus from Sri Pada Vallabha and Narasimha Saraswathi, the incarnations of Lord Dattatreya, up to our present Guru, Sri Swamiji.

    1. 01 Lord Dattatreya Adi Guru
    2. 02 Sripada Vallabha First manifestation of Lord Datta
    3. 03 Narasimha Saraswati Second incarnation of Dattatreya
    4. 04 Sri Ganapathy Sachchidananda Swamiji Our present Guru

    Ashram life

    A day at the Peetham

    Upon arrival, visitors are enveloped in a serene and pure atmosphere, resonating with the soothing chants of Vedic hymns. Visitors are encouraged to join a guided tour through the Ashram to fully immerse themselves in its serene ambiance.

    1. 01

      Temple opens · 6 AM

      Daily Abhishekam, Archana and Ashtottara Seva are performed to Lord Dattatreya.

    2. 02

      Sri Chakra Puja · about 8 AM

      When in town, Sri Swamiji or Sri Balaswamiji performs it. Teertha is offered, and devotees practise Datta Kriya Yoga.

    3. 03

      Rituals & lectures

      Devotees can join and sponsor rituals such as Homa and Tailabhishekam. Sri Balaswamiji gives daily lectures on the Yoga Vasistham in Kannada and Telugu.

    4. 04

      Evening bhajans

      Divya Naama Sankeertana for at least one and a half hours, and Kakada Arati with sixteen lamps before Lord Datta.

    On Sundays large numbers of devotees come from great distances to sing with Sri Swamiji. When He is in town, Sri Swamiji gives darshana on Sunday mornings, and Tailabhisheka (oil anointing) is performed to Kaalaagni Shamana Datta every Sunday.

    The festival year

    Maha Shivaratri, Sri Swamiji’s Birthday, Navaratri and Datta Jayanti are the most auspicious events of the year — each a blend of Vedic rituals, classical music, dance and traditional drama that becomes one holistic act of worship.

    Set by the Hindu calendar

    Maha Shivaratri

    Rudra Abhishekam to Sri Sachchidanandeshwara, music until late night as devotees keep vigil, and at 3 AM Sri Swamiji’s discourse on Shiva.

    26 May

    Sri Swamiji’s Birthday

    Pratyaksha Paada Pooja by Sri Balaswamiji, wheelchairs and walkers for the physically challenged, and Annadana on a large scale.

    Sep or Oct · nine days

    Navaratri

    Nava Avarana Puja to Mother Raja Rajeshwari, Kumari Puja, Devi Homa and concerts in Nada Mantapa.

    December · three days

    Datta Jayanti

    The incarnation day of Adi Guru Dattatreya — the one day of the year devotees may offer oil ablutions to the Moola virat.

    Sri Datta Humane Services

    The volunteers of SDHS

    SDHS is a volunteer corps dedicated to the service of Sri Swamiji, His Ashrams and related social programmes in India and abroad. Its volunteers serve pilgrims during major socio-religious events and the needy during natural calamities.

    Where we are

    In the Royal City of Mysuru

    The Ashram stands at the footsteps of Chamundi Hill in the historical city of Mysuru, also hailed as the cultural capital of Karnataka.

    Persons of all faiths are welcome. Facilities are limited, so visitors are requested to stay in hotels in the city and come to the Ashram each day for its programmes.

    • AddressSri Ganapathy Sachchidananda Ashram, Datta Nagar, Ooty Road, Mysuru, Karnataka 570 025, India
    • Temple darshanMorning 6:00 AM – 12:00 PM · Evening 4:00 PM – 8:00 PM
    • By city busBus 119 runs from the Mysuru City Bus Stand directly to the Ashram.
    • From abroadFly to Bengaluru, then take a taxi to Mysuru. Road travel from Bengaluru after 9 PM is not advised.
